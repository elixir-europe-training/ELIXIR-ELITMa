In [Chapter 3]({{ site.baseurl }}/03-dm-content), you identified the main areas of your Node data management strategy and drafted a first outline. In this chapter, you look more closely at selected areas. With the Data Stewardship Handbook Maturity Model, you find out what is already in place, how well it is developed and where there is room for improvement.

## Why assess your current situation

Before you decide what to develop further, it helps to know where you are now. An assessment makes existing work visible. It can show that an area is already well developed, that practices differ across the Node or that something depends heavily on one person, project or institution.

It can also help you tell different situations apart. For example, you may find that:

* a service or practice already works well and people know where to find it
* something exists, but it is hard to find, poorly coordinated or used in different ways across the Node
* an area is still developing, and practices or roles are not settled yet
* another organisation provides it, or it falls outside the Node's current scope

These differences matter. Not every area needs to reach the same level of maturity, and a lower maturity level does not automatically mean that something is wrong or missing.

## Using the Data Stewardship Handbook Maturity Model

The [Data Stewardship Handbook Maturity Model](https://elixir-europe.github.io/ds-handbook/maturity-model) is a self-assessment tool for exploring how research data management is organised and supported. It has four domains: strategy and sustainability, legal and governance, RDM support, and data and metadata management.

Each domain contains indicators, on areas such as RDM strategy, personnel, funding, training, communication, guidelines, services, infrastructure, standards and data management planning. For each indicator, the model describes three to five maturity levels. Use these descriptions to discuss which level best reflects your current situation.

{% include callout.html type="important" content="The levels help you start a discussion about where your own Node stands, without ranking it against other Nodes." %}

## Connecting the Maturity Model to your strategy

The GORC model you used in [Chapter 3]({{ site.baseurl }}/03-dm-content) and the Maturity Model complement each other, but they do not map one to one. A broad strategy area may connect to several indicators, and one indicator may be relevant to more than one area.

For example, a strategy area on **human capacity** could connect to indicators on personnel, training, training materials, expert networks and communication. An area on **sustainability** could connect to funding, personnel, goals, monitoring and the organisation of services.

{% include callout.html type="tip" content="Start small. You do not need to map the two models completely. A few indicators for one or two strategy areas are enough to begin with." %}

## Reflection: choose what to assess

Return to the strategy outline you created in [Chapter 3]({{ site.baseurl }}/03-dm-content) and choose one or two areas that you want to understand better. Good candidates are areas where several institutions or teams contribute, or where practices differ across the Node. An area is also worth a closer look when the current situation is unclear, or when people have already discussed improving it.

For each selected area, look through the Maturity Model and identify one or more indicators that can help you assess it.

| Strategy area | Relevant Maturity Model indicator | Why is this useful to assess? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add an area from your strategy outline | Select one or more relevant indicators | Note what you want to understand better |

Do not worry if there is no exact match. Select the indicators that help you ask useful questions about the current situation.

## Exercise: assess selected areas

Now use the maturity descriptions to assess the indicators you selected. For each indicator:

1. **Read the maturity descriptions**  
   Which description comes closest to the situation in your Node? If no single description fits, write down which parts match and which do not.

2. **Check the evidence**  
   What makes you choose this description? Think about existing documents, services, roles, practices, feedback or examples.

3. **Compare perspectives**  
   Would people from different institutions or roles give the same answer?

4. **Record what is working**  
   Which parts are already clear, established or useful?

5. **Note what could improve**  
   Where is there inconsistency, limited visibility, unclear responsibilities or another issue worth exploring?

Use a simple table to capture the discussion.

| Strategy area | Maturity Model indicator | Current situation | What is working | What could improve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Add your selected area | Add the indicator | Note the maturity description that best fits and why | Record strengths or existing practice | Note gaps, uncertainties or possible improvements |

## Assessing a distributed Node

Many ELIXIR Nodes are distributed. They bring together several institutions, each with its own policies, services and level of RDM maturity, so the answer to an indicator may differ between institutions. One institution may have a mature service while another relies on informal support. A national infrastructure may provide something the Node itself does not, and some activities may sit entirely with individual institutions.

Guidance can also diverge between levels. An institution's own policy may point researchers to one repository and the Node's guidance to another, for example an institutional Dataverse instance and ENA for sequence data. The Node is often not a separate legal entity, so it cannot simply overrule institutional policy. Do not force these differences into a single answer, but record them, for example like this:

| Indicator | Current situation |
| --- | --- |
| RDM training | Regular training is available through two partner institutions, while other Node partners mainly use external training |
| RDM information / guidelines for researchers | Several institutions provide their own guidance, but there is no shared Node overview |
| RDM personnel | Data stewardship expertise exists across the Node, but responsibilities and capacity differ between institutions |
| Research data publication | Institutional policy and Node-level guidance sometimes point researchers to different repositories, for example Dataverse versus ENA |

This can reveal useful questions about coordination without assuming that every activity needs to be provided at Node level.

## What your assessment could look like

Imagine that **human capacity** is one of the areas you selected. You might assess it with two indicators:

| Strategy area | Maturity Model indicator | Current situation | What is working | What could improve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Human capacity | RDM personnel | RDM duties exist across several institutions, but there is no shared overview of roles or capacity | Expertise is already present across the Node | Responsibilities and available capacity are difficult to see |
| Human capacity | RDM training | Several partners provide training, but provision varies and activities are not always connected | Existing courses and trainers can be reused | Better overview and coordination could make training easier to find |

The result does not yet tell you what the Node should do next. It gives you a clearer picture of the current situation and possible areas to discuss further.

## Reflection: what did the assessment show?

Look back at the indicators you assessed and the notes you made, and consider:

* What already works well, and where do institutions or people see things differently?
* Which activities exist but are hard to find, have no clear person responsible, or depend on one person or a temporary arrangement?
* Did something you saw as a gap turn out to be available elsewhere?
* Which possible improvements came up in the discussion?

Write your answers in your strategy outline, next to the areas they belong to.
