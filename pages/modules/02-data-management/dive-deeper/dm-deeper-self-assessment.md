This case study draws on presentations and discussions from the Espoo edition of the ELIXIR Node Data Management Strategy (NDMS) module in 2023. The original training materials and shared notes are available through the [ELITMa Espoo workshop materials](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10895080).

During the workshop, participants explored an early version of an RDM services maturity model. It covered areas such as strategy and sustainability, legal aspects, support, and data and metadata management. For each area, participants could compare their current situation with descriptions of different stages of development. This helped them discuss what was already well developed and where there was room for improvement.

## Why is this relevant?

Assessing your current situation can easily start to feel like scoring or comparing performance. Participants in Espoo saw it differently. They described the model as a tool for self-assessment, and even suggested that “profile” might be a better name than “maturity model”. For them, the aim was to understand where a Node stood and to use that as a basis for further discussion.

## What can Nodes learn from this?

- **Self-assessment starts a discussion.** It helps you see your own situation and talk about it in a structured way.
- **Different areas can be at different stages.** Training may already be well established while policies are still developing.
- **Not everything has to sit within the Node.** Partner institutions or other infrastructures may provide support, and some areas fall outside the Node's scope.
- **Differences are useful information.** Strengths and gaps, and the variation between institutions, show what the situation looks like in practice.
- **Assessment comes before prioritisation.** First find out where you are, then decide where you want to go.

## What can you do?

When you review your own assessment, look across the areas you selected and discuss what stands out. You may find areas that already work well, differences between institutions or services, or things that could be stronger. Record these observations without turning them into priorities or actions yet. For now, the aim is a shared picture of where you are, which is your starting point for choosing a direction in [Chapter 5]({{ site.baseurl }}/05-dm-direction).
