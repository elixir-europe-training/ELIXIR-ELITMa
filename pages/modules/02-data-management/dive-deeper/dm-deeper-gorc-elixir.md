ELIXIR is a distributed research infrastructure. Its Nodes bring together services, resources, expertise and communities across national and European contexts. The [Global Open Research Commons (GORC)]({{ site.baseurl }}/dm-deeper-gorc-framework) framework offers a way to see these elements as parts of one connected system. It can help Nodes understand how their activities fit together, where coordination is needed and which areas belong in a Node data management strategy.

## Why is this relevant?

ELIXIR activities are spread across Nodes, Platforms, Communities and the Hub. Different structures plan, fund and deliver them. A service rarely stands alone. It depends on infrastructure, standards, expertise, governance and funding, and you miss that if you only look at the service itself.

Some resources are recognised for ELIXIR as a whole, such as [Core Data Resources](https://elixir-europe.org/platforms/data/core-data-resources) and [FAIR-Enabling Resources](https://elixir-europe.org/platforms/interoperability/rirs) (FERs, formerly Recommended Interoperability Resources). Independent reviewers select them through an ELIXIR-wide process. So even when one Node runs them, they are more than that Node's own tool. [Life Science Login](https://lifescience-ri.eu/ls-login/), which replaced the ELIXIR AAI in 2022, is a shared login service that others build on.

## What can Nodes learn from this?

GORC gives Nodes, Platforms and Communities common terms for work they would otherwise describe differently. That makes it easier to see where a priority depends on other elements. It also helps to separate what the Node delivers itself from what it coordinates or shares with others. Nodes differ, so their strategies will too: GORC offers a common structure, not the same priorities.

Whether your Node runs such a resource or uses one, it belongs in your strategy. A Node that runs one should show how it supports the resource in the long term. A Node that needs something similar can first check whether one of these resources already does the job, so it does not build the same thing again.

## What can you do?

Use the GORC elements to sort your activities into broader areas, link them to ELIXIR-wide structures and spot what is fragmented or missing. Then pick one priority from your Node context and ask yourself:

- What does it depend on: infrastructure, standards, people, governance or funding?
- What does your Node do itself, and what do others do, such as Platforms, Communities, the Hub or external partners?
- Is it clear who is responsible, or is more coordination needed?

Check that this priority also shows up in your Service Delivery Plan (SDP), where the Node sets out the services and resources it offers. Use the result to refine your strategy outline.
