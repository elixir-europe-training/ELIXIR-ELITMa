This page draws on presentations, Node portfolios and discussions from two editions of the ELIXIR Node Data Management Strategy (NDMS) module: [Espoo in 2023](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10895080) and [Barcelona in 2026](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23575007).

The workshops showed that Nodes can be in quite different situations when considering how Research Data Management (RDM) services are supported over time. We only name Nodes where their example is published elsewhere. Funding changes, and Nodes are still developing their strategy.

## Why is this relevant?

RDM services may be supported in different ways and the model can change over time. Much of the work is paid for by time-limited projects. Institutions or the government sometimes add structural funding. In Barcelona, several participants said that much of what is in place now is not sustainable.

Funding also shapes your strategy. You need enough of it to deliver the data management services you describe. Even a running service needs money, for example for technical backup or an editorial board. A strategy can also be the reason to make good RDM work that already exists more sustainable. For one participant, sustainability should be one of the main topics of the strategy.

## What can Nodes learn from this?

The examples point to several approaches that may contribute to sustaining RDM support. Most of them are about money. How do Nodes put together enough of it to keep their services running?

- **Where the money comes from.** Projects can provide funding for developing or delivering RDM activities over a defined period. Sometimes these are large national projects that run for several years. RDM activities can also be supported through contributions from the institutions involved in a Node. Structural public funding may come from a national ministry or a research council. A private foundation paid for long-term support elsewhere.
- **A combination of sources.** One funding source does not necessarily cover everything. Different sources may support different activities or periods. The longer-term model is often still developing. A Node in Espoo expected to combine project and institutional funding in the long term. Service fees might come later.
- **Cost recovery.** Several Nodes recover part of their costs from users. A first consultation is free. Short- or medium-term support is paid by the hour. Larger partner projects pay the full cost. Elsewhere, services are free of charge for national users. Data is hosted for free if it is shared with the wider research community. Anything else is priced case by case.

A second group is about the people who do the work, who often leave when the funding stops.

- **Contributions in kind.** In one Node, only the coordinating roles are funded. All other activities and commitments are in-kind contributions from member institutions. The sustainability of these activities and of the expertise depends on those institutions. Networks of data stewards depend on volunteering.
- **People on fixed-term contracts.** Many data stewards in local support hubs are on fixed-term contracts that are not renewed when project funding ends. The expertise leaves with them and researchers lose their contact. The hubs keep starting over.

The last group looks at what makes the services themselves easier to keep going.

- **A coherent portfolio.** When most services support research data management and build on each other, even a small Node can offer a broad range of them. If services have little in common, each one needs its own effort to keep going.
- **The Service Delivery Plan.** Adding a service to the Service Delivery Plan was presented in Espoo as a step towards sustainability. The example was the FAIR Cookbook, which ELIXIR Luxembourg, Spain, Switzerland and the UK now list as a [service](https://elixir-europe.org/services). Services in the plan become ELIXIR Services. That makes them eligible for ELIXIR funding. ELIXIR also expects you to review them regularly and to decide when to stop one ([ELIXIR Node Service Selection Process](https://elixir-europe.org/platforms/interoperability/guides/node-service-selection-process)). At the same time, some Nodes were reluctant to list services because the expectations were unclear. Italy and Germany now use documented criteria to decide which services go into their plan.
- **Infrastructure Services.** ELIXIR funds a small set of [Infrastructure Services](https://elixir-europe.org/how-we-work/scientific-programme/technology). Examples are the ELIXIR AAI for logging in and TeSS for training. Many ELIXIR activities depend on them. The funding pays for running and maintaining these services. Money for new features has to come from other sources.

A few Nodes are still looking for official commitment and initial funding for a first position. For them, the first step may be to connect with government and national funders. The strategy comes after that. Nodes that are further along ask how they can justify more funding.

The workshop examples did not point to one model that all Nodes should follow. Sustainability looks different depending on the national and institutional context.

## What can you do?

Look at how your current RDM activities are supported. Which activities depend on project funding? Which have institutional or structural support? Are several sources combined? Who is on a fixed-term contract? And which activities still need a longer-term solution?

Making these differences visible can help you see where continuity is already supported and where sustainability still needs attention.

Then use what you found in your strategy. Add a review point for each activity that depends on a project. Set it a few months before the funding ends. That gives you time to look for other funding or to plan a handover. [Chapter 7]({{ site.baseurl }}/07-dm-sustain) shows what to do when funding ends or people leave.
