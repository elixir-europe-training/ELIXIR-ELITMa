In [Chapter 6]({{ site.baseurl }}/06-dm-plan), you turned your priorities into a practical plan. A good strategy is not something you finish and file away. It should help you notice when something changes, check whether you are still heading in the right direction and decide what to do next.

## Keep an eye on progress

Look at the actions in your plan and decide what would show that you are making progress. For example, a decision that has been made, or a service that people can now find. You only need enough information to notice when something needs attention.

Put the strategy on the agenda of a meeting you already have, such as the Node coordination meeting or an annual planning session. Ten or fifteen minutes is usually enough.

In that meeting, ask: does our direction still make sense, and do the right people still have the time and responsibility to take it forward? If yes, carry on. If not, update the part that no longer fits.

## When to revisit the strategy

Your strategy will need updates when things around it change. Funding ends and people move to other roles. New partners or initiatives may also change what is possible. When that happens, go back only to the part of the strategy that is affected. You might reassess one of the areas from [Chapter 4]({{ site.baseurl }}/04-dm-assess), or adjust an action from your plan. You do not need to start again from [Chapter 1]({{ site.baseurl }}/01-dm-intro).

A change can also shift who makes decisions or who has time for the work. Check whether it is still clear who is responsible for what. If it is not, agree on this before the work continues.

## Make sure it survives a handover

A strategy is fragile if only one person knows where it is, why choices were made or what should happen next. This matters even more when much of the work depends on fixed-term contracts or project funding. When people leave, their knowledge can leave with them.

Keep the latest version somewhere people can find it. Record important decisions and changes briefly, including why they were made. Then a new colleague can quickly see what the Node is working towards, what has already been agreed and where they may need to contribute.

{% include callout.html type="tip" content="Make responsibilities and the review routine visible to more than one person, for example with review dates in a shared calendar. Continuity should not depend on one colleague remembering what happens next." %}
